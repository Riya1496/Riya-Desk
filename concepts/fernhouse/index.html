01 — The brief

Fill the first classes of a new studio.

Purpose Agree what the page has to do before designing anything.

The business

A new reformer studio

Small groups, one neighbourhood, opening soon. It needs a steady flow of first-time visitors who stay on as members.

The ask

One page for paid traffic

A landing page that ads and the Instagram bio can point to, separate from the main website.

Success looks like

Booked and attended

Not clicks or page views. A first class that is booked, attended, and leads to a second visit.

02 — Audience

Curious, but not sure it’s for them.

Purpose Know who lands on the page, and what stops them from booking.

Primary

The curious beginner

Working professional, 25–40, lives or works nearby. Has saved Pilates Reels for months but never tried it.

I’d love to try it, but I’m not flexible and I don’t want to look lost.
Wants
A gentle, guided start and to know exactly what happens in class.
Worries
Being the only beginner, hidden costs, a long commitment.
Decides on
Phone, mostly in the evening, straight from an Instagram ad.
Secondary

The gym switcher

Already works out but is bored of the gym, or wants something lower-impact alongside it.

Is it actually a workout, or just stretching?
Wants
A real challenge, good instructors and times that fit around work.
Worries
Paying boutique prices for something too easy.
Decides on
Schedule, location and a clear first-class price.

Personas are assumptions for this concept. In a real project, I’d check them against the studio’s enquiries and a few short customer calls.

04 — Insight

The fear isn’t the workout.

Purpose Find the one truth that the headline, offer and page should be built around.

The insightBeginners don’t fear the exercise. They fear walking into a room where everyone else already knows what they’re doing.

So the page shouldn’t sell Pilates. It should sell a safe first hour: a beginners-only class, a small group, an instructor who explains the machine, and a clear price. Once that fear is gone, booking feels easy.

05 — Strategy

One page. One offer. One action.

Purpose Remove every choice that isn’t “book your first class”.

01

Match the ad

The headline repeats the ad’s promise word for word, so the click feels like the right place.

02

Make the first step small

A low-cost first class rather than a membership. Ask for the smallest yes first.

03

Answer fear before asking

Show what happens in class and answer the beginner questions before the booking button repeats.

1

Reassure

“Never done Pilates? Perfect.”

2

Offer

First class, beginners only, clear price.

3

Explain

What happens in your first 50 minutes.

4

Answer

Flexibility, fitness, what to wear, parking.

5

Book

Pick a time. Name + WhatsApp. Done.

The offer
First class,
₹499

Beginners-only reformer class in a group of six, with grip socks included.

  • Beginners onlyRemoves the fear of being the only new person in the room.
  • Groups of sixSignals attention from the instructor, not a crowded class.
  • Price shown upfrontNo “DM for price”. A clear, low number is easy to say yes to.
  • Grip socks includedOne less thing to buy or worry about before the first visit.
  • Free rescheduleUp to 12 hours before, so booking doesn’t feel risky.

The price is a placeholder for the concept. In a real project it would be set with the studio, based on its costs and class capacity.

07 — Why visitors act

What makes someone book.

Purpose Name the levers on the page, so each one can be tested and improved.

Relevance

“This is for me”

The headline speaks to beginners by name. People act when the page clearly describes them.

Low risk

A small first yes

One class, a clear price, free reschedule. A small, reversible decision is easier than a big one.

Certainty

No surprises

The class timeline and FAQ show exactly what will happen. Uncertainty is what makes people delay.

Ease

Three taps

No app, no account, two fields. Every extra step loses some people.

Honest urgency

Real spots left

Small groups mean limited places, shown only when it’s true. No fake countdown timers.

Trust

Proof, when it exists

Instructor names and certifications at launch, then real member reviews once the studio has them. No invented testimonials.

Weeks 1–2

Set the baseline

Run the page as designed and record every metric above. No targets until there is real data.

Weeks 3–6

Fix the biggest leak

Find the step with the largest drop-off and test one change at a time.

Ongoing

Optimise for members

Judge ads and page changes by cost per attended class and second-visit rate, not by clicks.
