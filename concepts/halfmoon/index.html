One offer.
Three hooks.

A paid social concept for Halfmoon Tiffins, a fictional home-style lunch service for Bengaluru offices. Three ads sell the same offer through three different motivations: money, home and time.

Concept by Riya, founder of Riya Desk

Brand

Halfmoon Tiffins (fictional), home-style lunch delivery

Offer

First 5 lunches at ₹99 each

Channel

Instagram & Facebook feed and Stories

Goal

First orders that turn into weekly plans

Brief→Audience→Problem→Insight→Strategy→Creative→Test plan→Measurement
01 — The brief

Get strangers to try a lunch subscription.

Purpose Be clear about the one job the ads have to do.

The business

Home-style lunch, in steel

Everyday North and South Indian meals, cooked fresh, delivered to office receptions by 12:30. Steel tiffins are collected the next day.

The ask

Launch paid social

Three ad variations for one offer, so the team learns which reason to buy is strongest before scaling spend.

Success looks like

Trials that stay

Not the cheapest click. First orders that turn into weekly plans after the trial ends.

02 — Audience

Same lunch, three different reasons.

Purpose Each ad targets one group, so the hook can speak to exactly what that group cares about.

Ad A

The daily orderer

22–28, first or second job, orders lunch on a delivery app most days.

I spend so much on lunch and it’s not even good.
Cares about
Price per meal, value, not feeling ripped off.
Reached by
Interest in food delivery, deals, budgeting; office areas.
Ad B

The one who moved

24–32, moved to Bengaluru for work, lives in a PG or shared flat, misses home food.

I haven’t had proper dal-chawal since I left home.
Cares about
Familiar taste, less oil, feeling looked after.
Reached by
Recently moved, home-state interests, Hindi/regional food content.
Ad C

The lunch skipper

28–40, back-to-back meetings, often eats late, at the desk, or not at all.

By the time I think about lunch, it’s 3 pm.
Cares about
Not having to decide, reliable timing, no effort.
Reached by
Job titles (managers, leads), productivity interests, tech parks.

Segments are assumptions for this concept. A real project would check them with a short survey of early customers.

03 — Business problem

Food ads all look the same.

Purpose Understand why a new lunch brand gets scrolled past.

1

The default is an app

Ordering lunch on a delivery app is a habit. A new brand has to give a reason to break it.

2

Subscriptions feel risky

A stranger is asked to commit to a week of food they’ve never tasted.

3

Nobody remembers the ad

Most food ads show a plate from above and a discount. People scroll past without noticing which brand it was.

50% OFF
FLAT ₹100
50% OFF
BUY 1 GET 1

The typical food ad: a plate from above, a discount badge, “Order now”. If you can swap the logo and nothing changes, the ad isn’t building a brand. So these three ads each lead with a human reason, and the price comes second.

04 — Insight

Nobody wants to decide lunch again.

Purpose Find the shared truth, then the three doors into it.

The insightOffice lunch isn’t really a food decision. It’s a 1 pm problem people have to solve every single day.

Halfmoon removes that problem. But people feel it differently: some feel it in their wallet, some feel it as homesickness, some feel it as lost time. So the ads don’t argue about food. Each one opens a different door to the same relief.

Money“I’m overpaying for food I don’t even enjoy.”
Home“I miss food that someone made with care.”
Time“I don’t have the headspace to think about lunch.”
05 — Strategy

Change one thing. Learn one thing.

Purpose Keep everything fixed except the hook, so the results show which reason to buy is strongest.

Kept the same

So the test is fair

  • OfferFirst 5 lunches at ₹99 each
  • Landing pageOne page with the menu, delivery areas and order button
  • Visual systemSteel tiffin, warm light, serif headline, turmeric price pill
  • Format4:5 feed image + 9:16 Story version
  • BudgetSplit evenly across the three ads
Changes

The hook

  • Ad AMoney: “₹400 for a sad salad? Again?”
  • Ad BHome: “Tastes like someone packed it for you.”
  • Ad CTime: “Lunch: sorted before your 10 am stand-up.”
  • CopyBody text that supports that one reason
  • ImageThe moment that matches the hook
5 lunches · ₹99 each

Delivered to your office by 12:30. Weekly plans only start if you choose one. Steel tiffins collected the next day.

The price is a placeholder for the concept. A real offer would be set from food cost, delivery cost and the expected trial-to-plan rate.

07 — What I would test

Three rounds, one question each.

Purpose Learn step by step, so every rupee of spend teaches the team something.

Round 1

Which hook wins?

All three ads run to the same broad audience in office-dense areas, using a split test so each ad gets equal budget.

Changes: only the hook.
Decides on: cost per first order.
Round 2

Does matching help?

Each hook runs to the audience it was written for, against the Round 1 winner shown to that same audience.

Changes: audience + hook pairing.
Decides on: cost per first order + week-2 plans.
Round 3

Make the winner better

Keep the winning hook. Test formats: a short Reel of the tiffin being opened, a carousel of the week’s menu, and a customer-style video once real customers exist.

Changes: format and visual.
Decides on: cost per weekly plan.
AdHypothesisWe’re right if…We’re wrong if…
A · MoneyPrice pain is the fastest way to a first order.Lowest cost per first order in Round 1.Orders come cheaply but few people choose a weekly plan.
B · HomeEmotion builds the most loyal customers.Highest share of trials that become weekly plans.Lots of saves and comments, but a high cost per order.
C · TimeBusy professionals value reliability over price.Strong results with older, senior job titles in Round 2.Click-through is low, because “saving time” feels vague for lunch.

Set the stopping rule first

Agree the minimum spend and number of orders per ad before the test starts, so nobody calls a winner too early.

Change one thing at a time

If the hook and the image both change, you can’t tell which one made the difference.

Don’t pick on clicks alone

The cheapest click can bring the least loyal customer. Follow people through to week two.

Refresh before fatigue

When frequency rises and results drop, rotate in a new image with the same hook.

08 — Measurement plan

What I would measure.

Purpose Measure each ad all the way from the scroll to the second week.

Stop the scroll
3-second view rate (video) · reach & frequencyDoes the hook make people pause?
Click
Link click-through rate · cost per clickDoes the hook make them curious enough to look?
First order
Landing page conversion · cost per first orderDoes the offer close the deal?
Five lunches
Lunches actually delivered and eaten · feedbackDid the food live up to the ad?
Weekly plan
Trial → weekly plan rate · cost per weekly planThe real measure: which hook brings customers who stay?
Before launch

Set up tracking

Pixel and conversion events for “first order” and “weekly plan”, UTM tags per ad, and a “How did you hear about us?” question at checkout.

Round 1

Set the baseline

No targets until there is real data. Record cost per first order for each hook as the starting point.

After Round 2

Scale what lasts

Move budget to the hook with the lowest cost per weekly plan, not the lowest cost per click.
