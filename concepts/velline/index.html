01 · The brief

Admired online. Forgotten after one visit.

PurposeAgree what the journey must change before designing a single touchpoint.

The business

A private atelier

Four suites, senior stylists and skin specialists, by appointment only. The experience in the chair is excellent.

The challenge

Two leaks

People admire the Instagram but rarely book. Many first-time clients enjoy their visit and then don’t come back.

The ask

A journey worth the price

Make every step before and after the appointment feel as considered as the appointment itself.

02 · Audience

They don’t need a salon. They need to be understood.

PurposeKnow what a high-end client expects, and what makes them hesitate.

Primary

The discerning client

35–50, senior professional or business owner. Already spends well on beauty, travels, and has been let down by salons that didn’t listen.

I’m happy to pay. I’m not happy to explain my hair from scratch every time.
Values
Expertise, privacy, being remembered, time used well.
Hesitates
When a DM gets a price list, or booking takes three calls.
Decides by
Instagram, then a trusted friend’s recommendation.
Secondary

The occasion client

Booking for a wedding, a milestone or a visit home from abroad. A single, important appointment.

It has to be perfect, and I can’t afford a trial that goes wrong.
Values
Certainty, a consultation first, a clear plan.
Hesitates
When there’s no way to meet the stylist before the day.
Could become
A regular, if the first experience feels personal.

Personas are assumptions for this concept. In a real project they would be checked through client interviews and booking data.

04 · Insight

What luxury clients are really paying for.

PurposeFind the truth that every touchpoint should be built around.

The insight At this price, clients aren’t paying for a haircut. They’re paying to be known.

The chair is already excellent. What’s missing is memory: someone who knows her hair history, her preferences and when she should come back, and who reaches out at the right moment without ever discounting.

05 · Strategy

Concierge, not customer service.

PurposeFour principles, then a new journey built on them, step by step.

i.

A person, not an inbox

Every client has a named concierge who replies, books and follows up.

ii.

Consult before you quote

The first reply asks about her hair goals. The price comes with a plan, not a PDF.

iii.

Remember everything

Hair history, formulas, preferences, even her tea. Recorded once, used every visit.

iv.

Privilege, never discounts

Loyalty earns access (priority times, private evenings), never a lower price.

1
DiscoverA feed with a door

Posts explain expertise (“Why your colour fades by week 6”). Highlights for The Consultation, The Atelier and Meet the Stylists. Bio: “Begin with a private consultation.”

Admiration needs an invitation, not a price.

2
EnquireA concierge replies

Within working hours, a named concierge replies, asks about her goals and offers a complimentary 15-minute consultation, in the atelier or by video.

The first reply sets the price expectation. Make it feel like care.

3
BookOne private link

Choose stylist and time in one step, then a short “Before we meet” card: hair history, goals, and how she likes her visit (quiet or conversation, tea or coffee).

Every question asked now is one she won’t answer in the chair.

4
ArriveExpected

Greeted by name. Her drink is ready. The stylist has read her card and opens with her goals, not a menu.

Being expected is the first luxury.

5
DepartThe next visit, held

Before she leaves, the stylist suggests the right return date and holds that slot for 48 hours. She leaves with a small aftercare set and a handwritten card.

The best time to book the next visit is while she loves the result.

6
Day 2 & week 5Care, then a nudge

Day 2: a personal WhatsApp from her stylist with care notes. Week 5: “Your colour is at its best until about week 6. I’ve kept two times for you.”

A reminder that sounds like expertise, not marketing.

7
Third visitThe Velline Circle

An invitation to the private client circle: priority times, a seasonal evening at the atelier, a birthday ritual, and first access to new specialists.

Loyalty rewarded with belonging, so the price never has to drop.

First month

Record the baseline

Track today’s journey with these metrics before changing anything. No targets without real data.

Months 2–3

Fix the two leaks

Introduce the concierge reply and the held next slot first. They address the two biggest drop-offs.

Ongoing

Grow the Circle

Judge success by return visits and repeat revenue, not by the number of new followers.
