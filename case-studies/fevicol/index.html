01 — The campaign

A glue brand that got stuck in the comments.

For Friendship Day, Fevicol and its agency Schbang brought the brand’s famous Mazboot Jod (strong bond) promise to life in the most literal way: by getting its comments “stuck” to other brands’ posts.

Fevicol left custom, witty comments across 100+ brand pages, including Netflix, Zomato and McDonald’s, and many of those brands pinned Fevicol’s replies to the top of their posts. The comment section became the campaign.

02 — Why it worked

The format was the message.

Product truth

A pin is a bond

A pinned comment literally stays stuck to the top of a post. The platform feature did the product demo, with no glue in sight.

Borrowed stages

Other brands’ audiences

By showing up on 100+ brand pages, Fevicol reached communities it could never buy its way into, at almost no media cost.

Shared spotlight

Friendship, in public

Every pin was a small public act of brand friendship: the theme of the day, played out between brands rather than claimed in an ad.

03 — My reinterpretation

If I took it further.

I asked what the campaign would look like as a single, quiet visual idea, and what a new wave of comments could say. My answer is a poster built on one image, two speech bubbles glued together, and a set of pinned-comment exchanges written to feel like friends finishing each other’s sentences.

04 — What I took away

Use the platform as the product.

A familiar platform feature can become a memorable brand experience when it connects directly to the product’s promise.

01

The best social ideas don’t need new formats. They give an old feature a new meaning.

02

Showing up on other brands’ pages turns their audience into yours, for a moment.

03

When the format proves the promise, the brand doesn’t have to say it out loud.
